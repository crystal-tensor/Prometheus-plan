# Can 412,920 exact checks still describe more than one integer polynomial?

Yes. R214 adds a sixth complete prime shard to the Prometheus-plan B9
characteristic-zero lifting route, and every declared exact check passes:

- the execution ledger advances to `18/42` determinant artifacts;
- minors 4, 5, and 6 again divide exactly by `D^44` modulo `536870837`;
- the primary and independent CRT paths each pass `412920/412920` residue
  round trips across eight total primes;
- custom modular Gaussian elimination reproduces `72/72` source-matrix
  determinants and `72/72` quotient recompositions;
- coefficient tampering and a missing frozen point are rejected by the
  immutable artifact grid.

The combined modulus grows from 194 bits to 223 bits. But the proved
worst-case uniqueness threshold is 428 bits, so multiple integer coefficient
vectors can still share the same observed residues.

That creates a useful design question: **when should a proof pipeline keep
buying certainty with more primes, and when should it attack the bound that
sets the price?**

Three contribution routes could materially change the schedule:

1. Derive support-aware coefficient bounds from the repeated `D^44` factor
   and the frozen degree-96 quotient supports.
2. Prove minor-specific uniqueness thresholds so one determinant can promote
   earlier without weakening the other two contracts.
3. Design a parallel shard protocol in which independent agents publish
   matrix replays and CRT receipts without learning the sealed holdouts.
