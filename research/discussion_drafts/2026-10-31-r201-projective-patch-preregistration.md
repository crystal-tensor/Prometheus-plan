# Can 184-Degree Minors Turn a Finite Bloch Atlas Into a Continuous Certificate?

R198 closed one continuous XZ meridian. R199 closed a second A-Y meridian.
R200 then survived 31 exact rational projective axes under four independent
modular primes. R201 asks the question those finite tests cannot answer:

> Does an extra quadratic-Majorana conserved operator appear anywhere between
> the sampled axes on a continuous two-parameter projective patch?

The engineering reconstruction is now exact. At `n=4`, the homogeneous
commutator matrix is `238 x 37`, contains `2,129` nonzero polynomial entries,
and has a maximal-minor degree bound of `184`. Eight public anchors, including
two points on the `c=0` projective boundary, all have full column rank. The
positive control still recovers the six known onsite charges.

That is encouraging, but it is not the proof.

The two unseen couplings `127/128` and `131/128` remain sealed. Before they can
run, the project requires an exact identity of the form

```text
D^q = A_1 f_1 + ... + A_k f_k
```

or an equally replayable ideal-membership trace. A Groebner-basis hash is not
enough. Neither is a finite grid, a modular rank loss, a numerical root, or a
failed SOS search.

The most useful contribution now is not another sample point. It is one of:

- a sparse rational Nullstellensatz certificate emitter;
- an independent CAS replay path that does not consume main-result-shaped
  inputs;
- an exact RUR/CAD fallback on `D=1`;
- a full-matrix algebraic kernel verifier for any candidate real root.

This preregistration grants no scientific promotion and no new credit. It
defines what must be proved before the frozen continuous experiment may even
begin.
