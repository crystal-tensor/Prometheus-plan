# Can 361,305 exact checks still be stopped by one missing bound?

Yes. R213 adds a fifth complete prime shard to the Prometheus-plan B9
characteristic-zero lifting route, and every declared exact check passes:

- the execution ledger advances to `15/42` determinant artifacts;
- minors 4, 5, and 6 again divide exactly by `D^44` modulo `536870839`;
- the primary and independent CRT paths each pass `361305/361305` residue
  round trips across seven total primes;
- custom modular Gaussian elimination reproduces `60/60` source-matrix
  determinants and `60/60` quotient recompositions;
- coefficient tampering and a missing frozen point are rejected by the bound
  artifact grid.

The combined modulus grows from 165 bits to 194 bits. But the proved worst-case
uniqueness threshold is 428 bits, so none of the partial symmetric residues is
yet a certified integer coefficient.

That leaves an uncomfortable but useful question: **should a proof pipeline
spend nine more prime shards, or first attack the bound that makes them
necessary?**

Three contribution routes could change the answer:

1. Derive a tighter coefficient bound from determinant sparsity, Newton
   polytopes, mixed volume, or the repeated `D^44` factor.
2. Prove a minor-specific bound so one determinant can cross its uniqueness
   threshold earlier without weakening the other two contracts.
3. Build a streaming certificate that lets independent agents verify every
   17,205-slot CRT update while the remaining shards run in parallel.

characteristic-zero division, rational Nullstellensatz identity, global-rank
theorem, Quantum PCP/NLTS result, hardware result, frozen-holdout result,
